# Voice & Tone — Najam Vozila

Controls how everything sounds. One home for voice: nothing here may be
duplicated into the source context.

## Core voice
Persuasive marketing blended with personal-blogging insider storytelling. Conversational, warm, direct, locally knowledgeable — "part fleet manager, part travel insider." Brand adjectives: clear (jasno), reliable (pouzdano), approachable (pristupačno), professional (profesionalno), stress-free (bez stresa). Default author persona: Marko Barić, a 15+ year Croatian rent-a-car industry veteran (Split-based) — field stories, direct opinions, no corporate hedging. Default to the strongest defensible version of an opinion rather than a hedged one.

Voice pronouns: "we/mi" for the brand, "you/vi/Vi" (polite "Vi" in more formal sections) for the reader; "I/ja" only when delivering first-person field experience or insider insight. Active voice throughout ("you pick up the car," not "the car is picked up by you").

## Reading level
B1–B2 Croatian: accessible to an average consumer and to foreign readers reading a translation. Short sentences, concrete figures, clear steps. Mixed expert-to-beginner (short-term consumer content) and more technical expert-to-expert register for B2B/long-term content. Average sentence length 10–15 words with deliberate variation (mix simple, compound, complex); never repeat the same sentence opener twice in a row.

## Style rules
- Translate every technical/insurance term on first use ("Translation:" / "In Plain English:" / "Here's what this means for your trip:").
- Never leave ambiguous pronouns ("it," "that," "this") — name the specific policy, fee, vehicle, or document.
- Use real, concrete Croatian scenarios and place names (routes, airports, cities, ferry lines) rather than generic statements.
- State the main keyword at or near the start of any sentence that contains it.
- Give measurements in Metric (Imperial) format, e.g. "410 km (255 miles)."
- Structural blocks to use where relevant: "What I Wish I'd Known," "Rental Insider's Truth," "Real Talk," "Before We Dive In...," "Under the Hood," "Road-Tested."
- Paragraph rhythm: vary length deliberately (mandatory short one-liners, one longer 6+ sentence paragraph, at least one sentence-fragment paragraph); no more than 3 consecutive paragraphs of similar length.
- Include exactly 3–4 controlled-imperfection touches per article (starting a sentence with "And"/"But," an em-dash interruption, one run-on sentence, a repeated word for emphasis, "thing/stuff" once, a "Look," opener, etc.).
- Do not end articles with a summary/conclusion paragraph — end on a forward-looking thought, question, callback, or single actionable line.
- Remove hedges ("in most cases," "it's worth noting," "it's generally recommended") in favor of direct statements; genuine uncertainty ("honestly, it depends on...") is acceptable, corporate hedging is not.
- FAQ answers at the bottom of articles: ~50–75 characters, direct and conclusive, no filler.

## Terminology & naming conventions (exact spellings, caps, hyphenation)
- Brand: **NajamVozila.com** (camel-case in logo; "NajamVozila" in running text).
- Use consistently: "najam vozila," "rent a car," "dugoročni najam," "kasko," "depozit," "neograničena kilometraža," "pomoć na cesti."
- Expand every acronym on first use in Croatian: CDW, TP, SCDW, franšiza (define immediately as insurance excess, not business "franchise").
- Every fleet model mention carries "ili slično" (or similar).
- Prices always in euros (€), VAT/PDV-inclusive, stated explicitly.
- Croatian diacritics (č, ć, ž, š, đ) mandatory throughout.
- Bilingual reality: Croatian is primary; English/German/Italian terms may appear for tourist-facing content but entity names must stay identical across language versions (hreflang-consistent), e.g. "Zračna luka Split (SPU)" ↔ "Split Airport (SPU)."
